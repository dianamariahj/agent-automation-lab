# Agent Types

AI agents can be designed in different ways depending on how much autonomy they have, how they make decisions, and how they interact with users and systems.

Understanding different agent types helps when designing workflows that are practical, reliable, and appropriate for real-world use.

---

## Reactive Agents

Reactive agents respond directly to input without long-term planning or memory.

They generally follow a simple pattern:

* Receive input
* Generate a response

These agents are useful for:

* Basic task assistance
* Question answering
* Simple automation

However, they do not plan ahead or adapt over time.

---

## Goal-Based Agents

Goal-based agents are designed to work toward a specific objective.

Instead of responding once, they:

* Evaluate the goal
* Determine the next step
* Continue until the objective is completed

These agents are commonly used for:

* Research tasks
* Workflow support
* Structured problem solving

---

## Planning Agents

Planning agents break down tasks into multiple steps before execution.

They typically:

* Create a plan
* Execute steps in sequence
* Adjust based on results

This approach is useful for:

* Complex workflows
* Multi-step processes
* Project planning

---

## Tool-Using Agents

Tool-using agents extend their capabilities by interacting with external systems and services.

Examples include:

* APIs
* Databases
* Search engines
* Ticketing systems

These agents can:

* Retrieve real-world data
* Update systems
* Trigger external actions

---

## Autonomous Agents

Autonomous agents operate with minimal human intervention.

They can:

* Plan tasks
* Make decisions
* Execute actions independently

In practice, fully autonomous systems are less common in enterprise environments due to security, governance, and oversight requirements.

---

## Human-in-the-Loop Agents

Human-in-the-loop (HITL) agents combine automation with human review.

A common workflow looks like:

1. The agent performs analysis or preparation.
2. A person reviews the output.
3. A person makes the final decision.

This model is widely used in:

* Cybersecurity
* Compliance
* Financial operations
* Business decision support

---

## Multi-Agent Systems

Multi-agent systems involve multiple agents working together to complete a task.

Each agent may have a specific responsibility, such as:

* Data collection
* Analysis
* Decision support
* Report generation

These systems can help:

* Divide complex work
* Improve scalability
* Simulate collaborative workflows

---

## Practical Considerations

Not every use case requires a highly autonomous agent.

In many situations, the most effective approach is:

* Simple agents
* Clear objectives
* Structured workflows
* Human oversight

The goal is not maximum automation. The goal is to create reliable, useful systems that support real work and produce consistent results.
